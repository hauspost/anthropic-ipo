## Founders' letter (inside front cover, 8 image pages a2026-10x25pg1a..pg8a.jpg, before TOC; unnumbered)
Signed: Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish, Chris Olah.

Structure: "four things that we would like any investor in Anthropic to understand: the incredible potential of AI, the risks we must address, Anthropic's unique approach to building a company and operating in the world, and the ways in which our behavior may differ from that of a purely profit-maximizing company."

Key verbatim lines:
- "We believe that AI is one of the most consequential technologies in human history."
- Scaling laws: "In 2020, four of our founders co-wrote a paper that documented the scaling laws of AI." ... "predictions made on the basis of the scaling laws have proved true again and again." Moore's-law analogy: "the impact of an exponential in machine cognition will be vastly greater even than that of the exponential in computer chips."
- "a country of geniuses in a data center"—millions of virtual agents ... "software, physics, math, biology, medicine, finance, economics, commerce, robotics, military and intelligence capabilities, and much more."
- Biology: "discovery of a molecular machine that might have the potential to be developed into a novel biotechnology tool, perhaps for gene editing."
- 2026 Ebola outbreak in DRC: WHO Africa used Claude to cut daily situation report from a full day to under an hour.
- "Doing so may well represent the greatest economic opportunity in history."
- Four risk categories: "misuse, loss of control, authoritarian abuse, and economic disruption."
- "AI may also behave autonomously in ways we (and our users) do not intend—as illustrated by recent high-profile cybersecurity incidents. Losing control of sufficiently powerful AI systems could be a global security threat of the first order."
- Economic disruption: "have already committed $200 million to partner with governments to run studies on different ways of responding to changes in the economy."
- Externalities framing: "AI has unusually large externalities, both positive and negative ... in tension with the natural incentives of a for-profit company, and with the standard governance structure of a publicly traded company."
- "Each Anthropic employee supports over $6.6 million in trailing 12-month revenue as of September 30, 2026"
- Donation match: "just over half of all our employees have pledged at least some of their equity." Footnote 2: pre-May-2024 employees offered match up to 50% of shares (over 70% pledged; over 25% pledged the full 50%); post-May 2024 match up to 25% (nearly 50% participated; nearly 30% pledged full 25%).
- "All seven of us have also pledged to donate 80% of our personal equity in Anthropic to charitable causes."
- "All seven of us still work at the company, which makes Anthropic the only major AI company to have retained its entire founding team. Over three quarters of our first 100 employees still work here" ... "19% subsequently returned to work at Anthropic."
- Governance: "shares power among three groups: our co-founders, our employees, and the Long-Term Benefit Trust". "The LTBT elects four of our seven directors, with one of those seats subject to safeguards on nomination and removal (including an employee vote if the company and the LTBT disagree). We, the co-founders, have the power to elect the other three directors. Our increased voting power is not permanent and voting power passes to our common stockholders once fewer than three of us remain in the voting entity."
- PUBLIC-GOOD EQUITY FUND: "Prior to our initial public offering, we are setting aside 5% of the company's equity for a fund devoted to the public good. We will set aside an additional 2% for every sustained 50% increase in share price from the prior contribution (starting with our initial public offering price), at our board's discretion." Footnote 3: in addition to founders' 80% pledges and employees' donation-matching pledges, "which amount to 9% and 8% of fully diluted shares as of June 30, 2026, respectively."
- "For investors concerned about dilution, we note that the size and structure of this plan are not outside the range of what has been offered as executive compensation packages at some public companies in the past."
- "race to the top"
- On investor expectations — advice heard: "never let your company be worth a dollar more than it is actually worth." ... "underpromise and overdeliver" ... "anti-sell".
- Two categories where decisions may run counter to investor expectations: (1) "business bets that derive from AI's wild exponential trajectory but seem strange or unorthodox in isolation", (2) "decisions that depart from profit maximization".
- Compute: "Anthropic will often make forward investments (especially in compute) that may seem irresponsibly large at first glance." Pharma analogy. "We will always be willing to forgo some upside in exchange for downside protection, for example by looking for ways to offload excess compute if we overestimate demand" ... "we will aim to be bold but sane".
- Product reinvention: "AI chatbots became viable around 2022, and AI coding agents around 2025; AI agents for general knowledge work and scientific discovery are gradually becoming viable in 2026." "less effort to entrenching our current products".
- Claude Mythos Preview: "we judged that its abilities, particularly in cybersecurity, had advanced to a point where releasing it widely without new safeguards would have carried significant risk. We therefore chose not to make it generally available and offered its capabilities to the public (as Claude Fable 5) only after we had built additional safeguards. The delay meant forgoing substantial revenue and ceding ground to competitors (we also gave away around $100 million in Claude credits to cyber defenders)".
- No ads: "We have also committed to keeping Claude free of advertising."
- Pace: "Building AI safely may require Anthropic (and ideally the rest of the industry) to modulate the extremely fast pace at which AI capabilities are compounding, especially when this is driven by AI systems building themselves. ... there may be times when we choose to advance more slowly than we could in order to make our systems safer, even if that puts us at a competitive disadvantage."
- "we encourage you, as a prospective owner of our stock, not to think of your purchase in purely transactional terms."
